# C1.1-P08 — Terminal, Reversible and Irreversible Outcomes

## Engineering question

Which outcomes can truly be undone, which cannot, and when must the business perform a new action instead of pretending history never happened?

## Terminal Order lifecycle states

Current P08 classification:

- REJECTED — terminal
- CANCELLED — terminal
- COMPLETED — terminal

Nonterminal:

- PLACED
- ACCEPTED
- PREPARING

Terminal means no further OrderStatus transition is currently defined.

It does not mean no workflow action can occur.

## Compensation after terminality

Example:

PAYMENT_RECORDED
→ ORDER_CANCELLED
→ REFUND_REQUESTED

OrderStatus after cancellation remains:

CANCELLED

REFUND_REQUESTED does not reopen the Order.

It also does not delete PAYMENT_RECORDED.

Both remain historically visible.

## Reversal versus compensation

Exact reversal would attempt to restore the prior world as though the earlier effect never happened.

Compensation performs a new business action whose purpose is to counteract or offset a prior effect.

Payment followed by refund is compensation:

Payment happened.
Later refund-related work happened.

The historical truth is not:

Payment never happened.

## Irreversibility

Some effects cannot be literally undone:

- recorded payment history cannot be erased merely because refund is requested;
- started preparation may consume ingredients/labor/time;
- completed fulfillment cannot be made historically "never completed" by changing a status backward.

A later business action may mitigate consequences, but that is not physical rollback.

## Order modification

P08 adds a real ModifyOrder use case.

Modification is currently allowed only while OrderStatus is PLACED.

Terminal Orders cannot be modified.

Important preserved pressure:

PAYMENT_RECORDED leaves OrderStatus PLACED.

Therefore payment does not yet block modification.

This is intentionally deferred for later modification-after-payment reasoning.

## Refund twice

P08 rejects a second REFUND_REQUESTED if one is already present.

This means two semantic compensation requests are currently disallowed.

This is not a claim of idempotent retry support because there is no command identity or deduplication mechanism yet.
