# C1.1-P14 — Cancellation / Acceptance Policy Alternatives

## Decision pressure

P13 proved conflict detection is needed if we want serializable lifecycle decisions.

P14 adds a separate business question:
what should happen specifically when CUSTOMER CANCEL and RESTAURANT ACCEPT overlap?

## Alternative A — First authoritative commit wins

Definition:
The first operation that successfully commits against the current PLACED version becomes authoritative.

The second detects conflict/current-state change and is reevaluated.

Benefits:
- simple;
- deterministic relative to authoritative commit order;
- compatible with optimistic version checking;
- avoids relying on client clocks.

Costs:
- infrastructure timing may decide a customer-visible business outcome;
- fairness semantics may be challenged.

## Alternative B — Cancellation priority before a business cutoff

Definition:
A cancellation registered before an explicitly defined fulfillment commitment boundary wins.

Benefits:
- intuitive customer protection.

Costs:
- "registered before" needs an authoritative ordering source;
- cutoff definition is domain policy;
- concurrent acceptance may already trigger restaurant work;
- time/network ambiguity becomes part of correctness.

## Alternative C — Acceptance priority after commitment

Definition:
Once restaurant acceptance reaches a defined authoritative commitment point, cancellation can no longer use the PLACED cancellation transition.

Later cancellation may require:
- special post-acceptance cancellation;
- fee;
- refund;
- compensation.

Benefits:
- matches real operational commitment.

Costs:
- richer lifecycle required;
- current P08 compensation/refund semantics may need extension;
- business policy must define what "commitment" means.

## Alternative D — Manual/conflict state

Definition:
Concurrent conflict enters a resolution state rather than silently choosing one actor.

Potential example:
DECISION_CONFLICT

Benefits:
- preserves ambiguity explicitly.

Costs:
- adds a lifecycle state and operational workflow;
- may be excessive for a problem solvable by ordering/commit semantics;
- no current business evidence justifies it.

Rejected for now as over-engineering.

## Current implementation

Accidental:
last write wins.

Not approved as business policy.

Both callers may be told success.

## Decision

P14 intentionally leaves business winner policy unresolved.

The evidence authorizes:
"we need a coherent winner semantics."

It does not yet authorize:
"customer always wins"
or
"restaurant always wins."
