# C1.1-P02 — Actors, Goals, Responsibilities and Use Cases

## Engineering question

Who is trying to accomplish what, and which responsibility belongs to whom?

## Why this comes before service decomposition

The business contains participants and responsibilities before it contains software services.

An actor is a business role or external participant that pursues a goal through the system. An actor is not automatically:

- a Spring bean;
- a database table;
- a bounded context;
- a microservice;
- a deployment;
- a team.

Likewise, a business responsibility does not imply a class or service with the same noun.

## Current actor model

### Customer — primary actor

Goal:

- place a food order that represents the customer's selected items for a restaurant.

Current responsibility represented in executable code:

- initiate the Place Order use case for the same customer identity represented by the actor context.

Business outcome:

- a new order is recorded in the current transient repository with status `PLACED`.

Important limitation:

- `ActorContext` is **not authentication**. The current HTTP adapter does not prove who the caller really is. It supplies the business actor semantics for the selected customer-facing flow only.

### Restaurant operator — discovered supporting actor

Goals expected from the roadmap-level workflow:

- decide whether an order can be accepted or rejected;
- later participate in preparation/completion behavior.

Current executable responsibility:

- none yet.

P02 intentionally demonstrates that a restaurant operator cannot execute the customer Place Order use case.

This does **not** create a Restaurant Service.

### Payment participant — candidate supporting actor

The roadmap requires payment/refund behavior later in Cluster 1.1. A payment provider or other external payment participant is therefore a candidate supporting actor.

Its exact responsibilities, commands, facts, authority and failure semantics are **not yet derived in P02**. They remain unresolved until later parts.

### Platform/operations participant — outside current business slice

Operational support may eventually need use cases such as investigation or manual recovery, but P02 does not invent those workflows.

## Non-actors

### Order

`Order` is a domain object with identity and state. It does not pursue a goal, so it is not a business actor.

### OrderController

The controller is an HTTP adapter. It is not a business actor.

### PlaceOrderService

The application service coordinates a use case. It is not the customer.

### OrderRepository

The repository is a persistence capability. It is not an actor.

## Actor → goal → responsibility → use case → outcome

For the implemented slice:

```text
Customer
  → wants food from a selected restaurant
  → is responsible for initiating their own order intent
  → executes Place Order
  → application records a PLACED order
```

This chain is intentionally semantic. It does not imply:

```text
Customer Service
  → Order Service
  → Restaurant Service
```

## Actor validity at the application boundary

P02 adds `ActorContext` to the Place Order command.

The application service verifies two current rules:

1. the use case is executed by a `CUSTOMER` actor;
2. the customer actor identity matches the requested customer identity.

Why enforce this at the application boundary rather than only in HTTP?

Because another adapter could otherwise call the use case while bypassing controller-specific checks.

This is still **business use-case eligibility**, not cryptographic authentication or a complete authorization model.

## Deliberately unresolved questions

P02 does not decide:

- how identities are authenticated;
- how permissions are stored;
- whether restaurant staff have individual vs restaurant-level identities;
- whether customers may place orders on behalf of another person;
- whether support staff can intervene;
- exact payment participant authority;
- cancellation policy;
- lifecycle transitions;
- bounded contexts;
- service boundaries.

Those are not gaps to fill casually. They are future learning pressure.
