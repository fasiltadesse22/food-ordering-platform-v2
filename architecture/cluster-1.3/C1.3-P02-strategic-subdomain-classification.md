## Central engineering question

**Which problems should count as core, supporting, or generic under an identifiable business strategy, and what evidence could disprove the classification?**

Our Java source can prove selected local behavior but cannot establish how this company wins customer preference. A strategic label is therefore a **conditional business hypothesis**, not something inferred from a package structure, commit count or number of SQL tables.

## Core, supporting and generic — working definitions

**Core subdomain candidate:** specialized business knowledge whose superior execution plausibly produces the firm's competitive advantage under an explicitly stated strategy.  
**Supporting candidate:** required business knowledge, possibly specialized/custom, that enables the product but is not the main differentiator.  
**Generic candidate:** recurrent market-wide problem for which standard products or implementations may satisfy requirements, even when business critical.  
**Unknown:** strategy/market/owner evidence is insufficient to justify a category.

Subdomain != bounded context != aggregate != Java module != independently deployed service. Important != distinctive. Generic != low quality. Buy-vs-build != classification. Category != SLO. These non-equivalences are required to protect interpretation.

## Hypothesis A: operations-led marketplace

**Assumption:** Merchant/customer choice depends primarily on reliable acceptance and capacity-aware fulfillment at peak demand.

- **Core candidates:** restaurant operating readiness, kitchen admission and truthful capacity-aware fulfillment promises.
- **Supporting candidates:** basic Order placement/lifecycle, ordinary merchant payout reporting, customer communications.
- **Generic candidates:** commodity financial rails, standard identity or message transport, *if* real vendor contracts satisfy non-negotiable requirements.
- **Unknown:** delivery ownership, Catalog pricing differentiation, exact inventory responsibility.

This is **not a statement** that a Restaurant Operations service exists, that customer complaints were measured, or that restaurant capacity decisions have already been implemented.

## Hypothesis B: merchant financial trust marketplace

**Assumption:** Merchant adoption and retention depend primarily on multi-rail payment outcome clarity, reconciliation and transparent settlement information.

- **Core candidates:** financial orchestration, merchant-facing reconciliation policies, ambiguous-outcome recovery UX/semantics.
- **Supporting candidates:** basic Ordering lifecycle, routine Restaurant coordination and Catalog presentation.
- **Generic candidates:** commodity external authorization/capture rails and standard message delivery; critical financial security/reliability still apply.
- **Unknown:** delivery ownership and Catalog differentiation.

This does **not** promote local `Order.recordPayment` into financial settlement; provider truth is not implemented at this checkpoint.

## Sensitivity of classification to strategy

| Business problem | A | B | Discriminating evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restaurant admission/readiness | CORE candidate | SUPPORTING candidate | Customer choice, refusal after acceptance and merchant capacity workflow |
| Payment orchestration & merchant reconciliation | SUPPORTING candidate | CORE candidate | Merchant churn causes, provider outage impact, desired settlement transparency |
| Commodity payment rail | GENERIC candidate, critical | GENERIC candidate, critical | Available provider functionality and risk obligations |
| Basic Order placement/lifecycle | SUPPORTING candidate | SUPPORTING candidate | Whether specialized policy, not ordinary CRUD, delivers differentiated advantage |
| Catalog quote/promotion policy | UNKNOWN | UNKNOWN | Merchant price agreement, differentiation, time-effective offer rules |
| Inventory reservation/admission | CORE/SUPPORTING undecided | SUPPORTING/UNNEEDED undecided | Whether marketplace promises inventory and owns stock admission |
| Delivery responsibility | UNKNOWN | UNKNOWN | Actual platform-vs-merchant operations and competitive differentiator |

## Decision and nonclaims

**Decision at P02:** keep the current single deployable and existing Order local consistency. Publish only a versioned strategic hypothesis and source-traceable gaps. Avoid premature Catalog, Restaurant, Inventory, Finance, Customer, Delivery or Notification microservices; do not infer business strategy from source. A real strategy/authority decision would authorize deeper domain implementation in a later part, accompanied by runnable tests and exact verification.

**No claim:** An experiment classifying two hypothetical strategies is not market research, a latency benchmark, CI success, customer data or operational evidence. Classifying an area as generic cannot weaken correctness/security. No production code/API/SQL/config changes are created by this document.
