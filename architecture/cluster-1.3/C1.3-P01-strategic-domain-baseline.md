## Central engineering question
Why is a correct aggregate model insufficient to establish the semantic architecture of an enterprise system?

## Traceable actual execution path
`OrderController.place` accepts `PlaceOrderRequest(customerId,restaurantId,lines)`, including caller-supplied menuItemId, name, quantity and unitPrice. It constructs `PlaceOrderCommand`; `PlaceOrderService.place` checks actorType CUSTOMER and actorId==customerId, constructs `Order.place` from provided commercial line values, calls `OrderRepository.createNew`, and returns `Accepted(OrderSnapshot, OrderPlaced)`. For configured PostgreSQL authority, `PostgresOrderRepository.createNew` writes root/lines/history atomically in local JDBC transaction. No production Catalog or price-authority lookup occurs on this inspected call path.
`Order.recordRestaurantAcceptance` checks local Order transition and matching assigned RestaurantId; it does not consult an operational Restaurant repository. `Order.recordPayment` records local PAYMENT_RECORDED occurrence; it does not prove provider authorization/capture/settlement. `OrderInventoryReservationPolicy.propose` returns a proposal from a potentially stale snapshot, not reserved stock.

## Four distinct correctness questions
1. Representation: Is input structurally valid? A nonnegative unit price can be structurally valid while not verified as an authorized restaurant price.
2. Aggregate consistency: Can Order's status, owned lines, local facts, and version be evolved and persisted coherently? Cluster 1.2 establishes selected controls, not exhaustive domain history.
3. Semantic authority: Who may determine current Catalog price, Restaurant operations, Inventory admission, external financial truth? These are NOT implemented as production authorities at the inherited checkpoint.
4. Business integration: Under what policy may a fact owned by one model influence another? This requires explicit scope, meaning, timing, translation and later consistency decisions.

## Distinctions and nonclaims
Aggregate != bounded context != module != service. Matching RestaurantId is an Order owner/actor guard, not proof the restaurant is open or the actor authenticated. A persisted OrderLine is historical/current Order-owned commercial content, not proof its price originated in an authoritative Catalog. A local PAYMENT_RECORDED occurrence is not provider settlement. An inventory plan is not allocation. Existing PostgreSQL transactions are local Order-state guarantees, not distributed business workflow atomicity. A successful local test is not a measured production SLO.

## Candidate responsibilities, NOT approved contexts
Ordering: lifecycle, Order identity, owned commercial lines/facts (implemented).
Catalog: current offerings/prices/publication (candidate; no authority implementation at P01).
Restaurant Operations: eligibility/readiness, staff operational decision (candidate; no authority implementation at P01).
Inventory: stock authority/admission (test-only admission examples; production pure planning).
Financial Operations: authorization/capture/settlement/refund (not implemented).
Customer, Delivery and Notification: further discovery required; no assumed deployables.

## Context-discovery hypotheses to test in later parts
Catalog's current offer changes independently of an accepted OrderLine snapshot; restaurant operational readiness differs from the Order's own acceptance state; financial authority is separate from an Ordering milestone; stock observation differs from an admitted reservation. Validate stakeholders' business rules before changing production workflows.

## Design choice for P01
Retain all existing source, API, SQL and topology. Add executable test-only negative controls for current price acceptance, restaurant identity guard, and payment milestone semantics. Capture unimplemented authorities and candidate context questions, do not rename Order or introduce Catalog/Restaurant/Payment modules yet.
